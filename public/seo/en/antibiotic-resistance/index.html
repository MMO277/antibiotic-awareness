What is antibiotic resistance?

How bacteria become less responsive to medicines, and why it matters to everyone.

General educational content awaiting review by a qualified professional before official publication.

Antibiotic resistance happens when bacteria change so that medicines that used to kill them or stop their growth no longer work as well. It is the bacteria that become resistant, not the person’s body.

Every time antibiotics are used, bacteria that survive can multiply. Using antibiotics when they are not needed, taking them incorrectly, or not following the prescriber’s instructions gives resistant bacteria more chances to spread.

Resistant infections can be harder to treat, may need stronger or longer treatment, and can spread to other people. Using antibiotics only when a healthcare professional prescribes them, exactly as instructed, helps protect these medicines for everyone.

For general education only; it does not replace a doctor or pharmacist. If you are worried about your symptoms, ask a qualified professional.
